# VAMS campaign direction: Design the Exit

## Job

Make the value of service continuity understandable to builders and technical organizations evaluating VAMS. Give operators and researchers a clear route to requirements and evidence. The site is the campaign surface; the public console and status pages are its verification paths.

## Core idea

**What survives when the host changes?** A service should retain its identity, authority, and recoverable state while its execution infrastructure can change. The question introduces the tension; the conceptual exit model shows the proposed architecture; the lifecycle explains the parts; status and research show what can currently be verified.

## Message hierarchy

1. **Question:** What survives when the host changes?
2. **Answer:** VAMS explores an architecture for services that preserve identity, authority, and state across infrastructure changes.
3. **Proof boundary:** Hardened pre-testnet candidate. Deployment pending. The exit model is conceptual, and live handoff is not claimed.
4. **Action:** Inspect the architecture; review the evidence. Builder, operator, organization, and researcher routes use specific verbs that match their destination.

## Visual system

- Keep the supplied VAMS lockup intact and preserve clearspace around it.
- Use the existing near-black, warm-white, editorial serif, reading sans, and mono-label system. Reserve amber for readiness warnings.
- Use one continuous service rail carrying identity, authority, and state between two distinct provider layers. The next provider remains conditional, indicated by a dashed edge and an evidence qualification.
- Keep the neural field as atmospheric depth. It is not a literal protocol topology or a depiction of deployed nodes.
- At every breakpoint, the question is the first visual read, the readiness state is visible, and the action follows without clipping or accidental overlap.

## Page sequence

| Surface | Role |
| --- | --- |
| Home hero | Pose the continuity question and direct visitors to architecture and evidence. |
| Exit model | Make the portability idea legible as a clearly labeled conceptual diagram. |
| Protocol, Network, Build, Operate, and Research | Repeat a compact exit-test thread with a page-specific question, three requirements, a deployment caveat, and a relevant next step. |

## Design gates

- **Claims:** No live failover, deployed node, economic action, or verified settlement claim without source-backed evidence. A conceptual diagram must say it is conceptual.
- **Brand:** No alteration to the official mark; no generic neon-network treatment that overtakes the continuity idea.
- **Copy:** Character-check the hero, readiness state, CTAs, and model caveat against the approved wording. Link labels must describe their destination.
- **Hierarchy:** One focal message per viewport; body contrast at least 4.5:1 and large text at least 3:1; no clipped text or horizontal overflow.
- **Responsive:** Inspect 320, 390, 768, 844 landscape, and 1440 widths. The first mobile action must remain within the initial 320×568 viewport; the landscape action must begin by viewport height plus 96px.
- **Motion:** The meaning, labels, and CTAs remain complete with reduced motion or WebGL fallback. Motion cannot imply an actual handoff.

## Current scope

This direction uses the repository's documented pre-testnet state and existing site assets. Timing, paid channels, budget, KPIs, and external brand guidelines were not supplied, so this document does not prescribe them.
